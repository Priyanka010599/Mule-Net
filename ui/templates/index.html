New here? How MuleNet works, in plain English

A money mule is an ordinary bank account used to pass criminal money along so its origin is hard to trace. Criminals link many mules into a ring and spread it across several banks, so every bank sees only a small piece and nothing looks wrong. MuleNet shows what changes when the banks' signals are put together, without any bank handing over its customers. Everything here is simulated data, not real customers.

  1. 1. Each bank looks alone

    Every bank scores only its own customers and sees only the transfers it is part of. One bank alone usually sees a harmless-looking fragment. That is this first tab.

  2. 2. The banks are joined

    The accounts and their links (who pays whom, shared devices, the same ID document used twice) are combined. Pieces that looked unrelated join into one ring. That is tab 2.

  3. 3. Two checks decide who is suspicious

    A simple speed rule flags an account that many accounts pay into and that pays the money on to several others within an hour. A learned model gives every account a suspicion score from patterns like these. Flagged accounts that are connected are grouped into a ring, with the reasons listed.

  4. 4. Privacy is kept while joining

    Accounts appear only as scrambled IDs, never names. The joining runs inside a locked Google Cloud environment (Confidential Space) that releases a bank's key only to the exact code the banks approved. Tab 3 has a live proof you can run.

  5. 5. We measure whether joining helps

    Tab 3 compares one bank alone against banks joined: how well real mules rank at the top (PR-AUC), how many innocent accounts are flagged per mule caught, and who flags a ring first. The race to the exit shows whether it was flagged before the money left.

  6. 6. Try to break it, or use your own data

    Tab 4 lets you design a transfer pattern to slip past the detector. Tab 5 scores your own files. The Ask button explains any result in words, and the case panel on tab 2 shows a checked report for a compliance team, with the rules cited, once a ring has been verified.
